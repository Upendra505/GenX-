ABOUT THIS SERVICE

What is FNAC & Biopsy?

Fine needle aspiration uses a thin needle to collect cells or fluid; a biopsy collects a small tissue sample. The recommended method depends on the site and clinical question, and should be explained by the treating clinician.

COMMON REASONS

When may it be used?

  • Evaluating a lump or selected tissue change when advised
  • Collecting cells or tissue for laboratory examination
  • Providing additional information after imaging or clinical examination
WHY IT CAN HELP

Potential benefits

  • A sample can be examined directly by a laboratory.
  • The method is selected for the area and clinical question.
  • Results may help the clinician decide on follow-up.
YOUR APPOINTMENT

What happens?

The clinician confirms the target area and explains the procedure. Depending on the procedure, imaging guidance or local numbing may be used. After collection, the sample is labelled and sent for analysis.

BEFORE YOUR VISIT

How to prepare

Instructions depend on the site, procedure and medicines you take. Tell the clinician about allergies, bleeding concerns and medications; do not stop prescribed medicines unless instructed.

Are FNAC and biopsy the same?

They are related but different sampling methods. FNAC collects cells or fluid with a thin needle; biopsy collects tissue. Your clinician can explain which is appropriate.
