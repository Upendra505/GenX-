ABOUT THIS SERVICE

What is Pap Smear?

The sample is collected by a trained healthcare professional using a small brush or similar device. A Pap test looks for cell changes; it does not by itself provide a complete assessment of health.

COMMON REASONS

When may it be used?

  • Cervical screening when recommended
  • Follow-up of a previous result as advised
  • Collecting a cervical cell sample for laboratory review
WHY IT CAN HELP

Potential benefits

  • A small sample can be examined for cervical cell changes.
  • Supports screening and follow-up conversations with a clinician.
  • The collection is typically completed during a brief appointment.
YOUR APPOINTMENT

What happens?

You will be asked to lie on an examination couch. A speculum is gently used to view the cervix and a small sample is collected. Let the clinician know if you are anxious or uncomfortable.

BEFORE YOUR VISIT

How to prepare

Tell the clinician if you may be pregnant or have had previous abnormal results. Ask about timing and any preparation instructions for your appointment.

What should I expect during a Pap smear?

A clinician collects cells from the cervix during an examination. Some people feel pressure or mild discomfort; tell the clinician if you need a pause.
